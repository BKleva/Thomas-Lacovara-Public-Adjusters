Whether by fire, flood, wind, there are 3 questions that often arise when someone experiences a major loss.

What Is A Public Adjuster?

A public adjuster is a highly trained insurance professional that represents you, the insured homeowner, with your insurance claim.

At Thomas Lacovara & Associates we are licensed by the New Jersey Department of Insurance and work to help you get the maximum amount of money you're entitled to receiving.

They are very interested in their bottom line and profits. We need to talk.

Why Choose A Public Adjuster?

The insurance company sends in their own adjuster that represents the insurance company, not you.

Insurance companies are part of major corporations and experts at paying the least amount of money to you for your damages.

They are very interested in their bottom line and profits.

When To Choose A Public Adjuster?

Immediately at the time of damage, you should contact a public adjuster. The first meeting with the insurance company is the most critical and important meeting — this is when the rules of engagement are established, identified as the Scope of The Loss.

The insurance company may want to bring in a restoration company right away. This is a huge mistake made by many homeowners, as you will lose tens of thousands of dollars just on that mistake.

Think of it this way: if you were in an automobile accident, would you clean and polish your car before the adjuster had an opportunity to review the extent of the damage?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Public Adjuster?

We represent you, the homeowner/insured, to make sure you receive the maximum amount of money that you're entitled to. We are licensed, bonded, and insured professionals — the insurance company represents the insurance company, not you.

Can't I just use the insurance company's adjuster?

The insurance company's adjuster represents the insurance company — not you. They are experts at paying the least amount possible.

Should my Public Adjuster be brought in at the beginning of a claim?

Yes. Most public adjusters work on contingency, typically charging 10% to 15% of the final settlement offer, with experienced adjusters potentially charging higher fees for larger, more complex claims.

Can I hire a Public Adjuster after the claim has already been settled?

Once the damage has been done, it's very difficult to reverse previous mistakes. We generally can't represent clients who have already attempted to handle their claim on their own.

How much does a Public Adjuster charge?

Fees normally range between 10% and 15% of the settlement. An experienced adjuster can increase your claim 50%, 100%, or 200% — more than enough to cover the fee.

Do I need a Public Adjuster?

It's the smart decision for any serious claim — the same way you'd hire an attorney for a serious legal matter.

What should I look for in a Public Adjuster?

Knowledge, experience, work ethic, honesty and reputation. Check references and verify their standing with the New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance.
